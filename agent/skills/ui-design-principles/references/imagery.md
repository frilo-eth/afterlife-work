# Imagery

Two sources: images the product chooses (marketing, hero, category art) and
images users upload (avatars, posts, listings). You control the first completely
and the second only through rules: aspect ratio, crop behavior, fallback.

## Photos

**A photo's job is to carry a story or an emotion, not to document an object.**
Cutlery laid out on a table shows the product. A family cooking together shows
why anyone would buy it. The second sells.

### Selection

- **One focal point.** If the eye has to hunt for the subject, the photo is
  decoration.
- **Fit the purpose.** A technically good photo of the wrong subject is a bad
  photo.
- **Real over stock.** Staged, over-expressive stock imagery reduces trust.
  Look for moderate expression and plausible situations.
- **Real people help.** Faces build trust and connection faster than objects.
- **Direct attention.** People look where the subject in a photo is looking.
  Point the gaze at the headline or the CTA.
- **High resolution in, compressed out.** Source large, hand developers several
  sizes. Never scale a small image up.

### Text on photos

Always add a scrim. Options, in order of subtlety:

1. Linear gradient from transparent to dark, covering only the text region.
2. Flat dark overlay across the whole image.
3. Brand-colored overlay, which doubles as a consistency device.

The image should still be visible through it. If the scrim has to be so heavy
that the photo disappears, use a solid background instead.

### Consistency

A set of photos from different sources will clash. Two fixes:

1. Apply a light shared color overlay or grade to all of them.
2. Spend the time sourcing images that already agree in palette and light.

Do not grade product photography in commerce contexts: buyers need the real
color of the thing they are buying.

### Aspect ratios

The same image will be cropped differently across breakpoints. Test at 1:1, 4:3,
16:9 and 4:5 before choosing it. If the subject falls out of frame in any of
them, pick another image or specify a focal point for the crop.

### Licensing

Free for personal use is not free for client use. Confirm the license before
anything ships, and tell the client explicitly when an image needs purchasing.
They routinely miss it.

## Illustrations

Illustration communicates abstract ideas that photography cannot. "Boost your
creativity" has no photograph.

### Where it works

- Landing pages, explaining what the product does.
- Onboarding, conveying the general idea rather than every feature.
- Empty states, paired with copy and an action that resolves the emptiness.
- 404 and error pages.
- Achievements and gamification.

### Where it fails

- **Real products.** A buyer wants to see the exact item. Photograph it.
- **Serious or luxury positioning.** Illustration reads playful and approachable,
  which fights a premium or high-trust message.
- **Small sizes.** Illustrations carry more detail than icons. Below roughly
  64pt, most stop reading. Use an icon or a simpler illustration instead.

### Consistency

Once illustration enters a product it becomes part of the brand. Use one set or
one illustrator. Mixed styles read as an unfinished product more loudly than
almost anything else.

3D illustration reads as premium and current when executed well, and as a
template when not. It is also the most expensive thing on the page in both
production time and load weight, so it needs a reason.
